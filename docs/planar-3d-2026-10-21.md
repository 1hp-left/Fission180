## Geometry and representations

`kernel/native/faces.rs` provides a reusable planar-face Cut/Join/Intersect path
for nonparallel polygonal extrusions, line-only occupied-section results and
earlier general planar results. Local orthonormal frames use actual outward
face normals, including oblique side faces. Analytic plane relations distinguish
parallel, coplanar and intersecting supports. Finite trimmed-face intervals decide
whether a plane intersection affects the operands. Face-local analytic planar
Booleans split retained regions; coplanar masks preserve concavity and holes.
Splitting can retain additional coplanar partitions rather than minimizing faces.

An interior witness comes from exact polygon scanline intervals. Closed-solid
classification uses oriented analytic ray/face intersections, including inward
cavity shells, with bounded alternative rays for trim-junction degeneracies.
Neither display triangles, voxels nor an axial sampling approximation decide
occupancy or output geometry. Operation-specific material-side selection removes
internal interfaces and selects a single copy of coincident boundary patches.

The assembler refines finite straight edges at shared vertices, shares edges and
vertices, and constructs opposed coedges. Each connected shell must pass loop,
incidence, orientation, edge-use, circular vertex-link and connectivity checks.
Analytic finite face checks reject transverse self-intersections and coplanar
interior overlap. Separate shells must be nonintersecting and correctly nested
with alternating signed orientation. Positive shells are material components;
their immediate negative children are void boundaries. An island inside a cavity
is a separate solid and does not steal the cavity shell from the surrounding body.

Positive-area face attachment may join material. Point/edge-only Join contacts
that create a nonmanifold boundary fail explicitly. Boundary-only Intersect is
empty. Coincident solids, full removal and separate material outputs are explicit.

`Representation` distinguishes uniform extrusion, occupied sections and general
planar faces. General shapes have no authoritative old extrusion silhouette or
section cache. Measurements integrate the actual oriented planar loops, including
signed inner-shell volume; bounds use the actual analytic vertices. Translation
moves the full B-rep and preserves its semantic transform ancestry. Display
triangulation is downstream and cannot change analytic measurements or identity.
Uniform-prism, occupied-section and legacy box paths explicitly exclude general
face results rather than retrying a different shortcut after a failure.

## Ancestry, persistence and bounds

New faces retain original `TopologyId` sources and semantic transform instances.
Directed planar trim intervals and endpoint/intersection relationships survive
splits, reversals and coincident merges. An inspectable source ledger retains
original edges and the original face relationships behind generated intersection
lines. Edge identities combine these junction relationships with incident face
identities; body signatures derive from retained face relationships. Coordinates,
rounding, allocation order and output-array positions do not name new topology.
Identity collisions fail explicitly, including source-ledger collisions.

Historical v5 rectangular split and disjoint Join outputs retain their bounds
ordering. Accepted output-count or ancestry changes still require explicit repair.
The `.ofrust` schema remains v5 with v1–4 reading; only sketches, parameters and
feature intent are saved, never meshes or opaque solid handles. Session/worker
tests cover Extrude and Combine, parameters, source-stable edits, changed-output
rejection, save/reopen, Undo/Redo and failed modeling/display transactions. Rust
RAII retains shape ownership on success and failure, including OCCT test oracles.

The worker also fixes a reproduced transaction defect: after a valid
topology-changing preview, a rejected OK previously supplied no replacement
display and could strand that draft onscreen after the editor closed. Rejected
Replace commands now return the accepted mesh, body information and backend.
Invalid Preview commands continue to retain the last valid preview. A dedicated
regression covers topology and injected meshing failures, history, the save path
and unchanged saved bytes. Existing rejection checks now assert the restored
accepted display rather than merely asserting an absent response payload.

Unchanged numerical policy: coincidence `1e-7 mm`, resolved separation `1e-5 mm`,
angular parallelism `1e-10 rad`, finite domain `±1,000,000 mm`. Unresolved cases
fail explicitly; no tolerance increase or healing was added. Limits include
2,048 planar face patches, 262,144 face pairs, 4,194,304 classification/refinement
work units, 16,384 edges/vertices, 64 shells, 512 ancestry sources per junction,
32,768 provenance records and 64 transform instances. Existing planar bounds of
512 curves, 32,768 intersection events and 8,192 spans also apply. A stricter
existing bound can be reached first.

## Remaining limits

The new general path accepts planar faces and straight edges. Plane–cylinder and
cylinder–cylinder intersection curves, arbitrary rotated sketch supports, splines,
revolve, fillets, chamfers, shell/offset commands and production Rust STEP exchange
remain unsupported. Existing same-axis curved operations remain available through
their explicit representation paths. Conservative near-resolution and bounded
resource rejection remain deliberate limits. Coplanar display seams, edge occlusion
and unmatched Fusion visual/workflow fidelity remain documented gaps. No pixel
perfection or Fusion feature parity is claimed.
Saved-document captures also retain the existing default Open camera, which can
show these small parts at a smaller scale than menu examples; this batch does not
redesign camera fitting or the viewport.
